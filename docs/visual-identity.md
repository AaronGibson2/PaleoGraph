# PaleoGraph visual identity

The **occlusal molar and raised atlas arc** connect vertebrate paleontology to place and exploration. A broad enamel rim and internal wear folds give the tooth its character; the open globe, curved meridian and evidence point extend it into an atlas. The compact proboscidean-inspired form also connects to the creator's related morphometrics work. Evergreen ink, mineral paper and the editorial wordmark carry it into the field-atlas interface.

The approved GPT Image study was converted to single-ink vector paths, preserving its tooth contour and raised arc. It is decorative brand artwork, not a diagnostic tooth diagram, specimen image, taxonomic identification or reconstructed phylogeny. No museum photograph is embedded in the assets.

- [Primary logo](../apps/web/public/brand/paleograph-logo.svg): mark and Georgia wordmark; text remains editable and depends on a serif font.
- [Standalone mark](../apps/web/public/brand/paleograph-mark.svg): transparent, traced vector geometry with enamel folds and the raised atlas arc.
- The shared `PaleoGraphBrand` component supplies the Home wordmark and compact workspace rail treatment. Parent links provide accessible names; the SVG is decorative.
- [Browser/app mark](../apps/web/app/icon.svg): the same light mark on an evergreen rounded square, served through Next's icon metadata.

Use the full signature where space permits and the compact mark in navigation. Preserve the wider proportions and clear space around the outer rim and arc; do not stretch the tooth into a square. Fine enamel detail naturally recedes at favicon sizes. Parent links retain the accessible PaleoGraph name, so recognition does not depend on interpreting the tooth. Avoid additional animation or repeated logos in scientific records.

## Introductory atlas

Home extends the opening field plate into an editorial collection gallery, an interactive Place / Material / Lineage study, source-context principles, and a creator colophon for Aaron Gibson. Gallery links open ordinary source search; the diagrams are labeled schematic and contain no invented records, ages, or map positions. Existing animal silhouettes remain decorative.

One-time section reveals, a scroll progress rule, enamel-mark branding, and hover/focus responses provide motion without scroll interception or a new animation framework. The study supports arrow, Home, and End keys. Reduced motion removes nonessential movement, including an active section reveal when the preference changes. Main content and exploration links render without JavaScript; the initial search never waits for the editorial effects.

## Decorative animal coverage

The renderer has 27 reusable body forms: the existing 14 transparent raster masks and 13 original filled vector silhouettes. New forms cover rays, salamanders, lizards, carnivorans, ground sloths, glyptodonts, eels, flatfishes, small insectivores, marsupials, angel sharks, pinnipeds and cetaceans. They share the existing single-ink mask treatment and fit dense rows without new image frameworks or network services.

Membership is explicit: reviewed UFVP source UUIDs and namespaced retained PBDB identifiers in `expanded-rules.ts`. Labels document the review; runtime resolution never infers membership from a name. The taxonomy projection supplies membership nearest first, so a broad neutral Reptilia node cannot hide a supported crocodilian or turtle. Additional specific forms override broader ancestors. Unknown and intentionally broad or unsuitable groups remain neutral.

These are orientation aids, not specimen photographs, exact species reconstructions, morphological evidence or proof of evolutionary relationships. Detailed source identities and uncertainty stay in the adjacent text. Image failures retain a neutral fallback. Hover is unnecessary for recognition, and reduced-motion behavior is unchanged.
